**Q1. (Short answer)** Cancer-predisposition genes are usually described as "dominantly inherited but recessive at the cellular level". Explain what this means using Knudson's two-hit model.

**Q2. (Multiple choice)** Which statement about BRCA1 vs BRCA2 carriers is correct?

- A. BRCA2 carriers have a higher ovarian cancer risk than BRCA1 carriers
- B. BRCA1-associated breast cancers are often triple-negative; BRCA2 is more strongly associated with male breast, pancreatic and prostate cancer
- C. Only BRCA1 causes homologous recombination deficiency

**Q3. (Short answer)** How do 3′ deletions of EPCAM cause Lynch syndrome?

**Q4. (Multiple choice)** A 68-year-old patient who had chemotherapy has a TP53 variant at VAF 0.06 in blood and 0.03 in the tumour. The most likely explanation is:

- A. Classic Li-Fraumeni syndrome
- B. Clonal haematopoiesis (CHIP)
- C. A clonal somatic tumour driver
- D. A sequencing error that should be ignored without review

**Q5. (Short answer)** Your somatic Mutect2 VCF has no BRCA2 variants, yet the clinical lab reports a pathogenic germline BRCA2 frameshift. Give two reasons this is expected, and what analysis you would add.

- A. BA1
- B. PP3
- C. PVS1
- D. BS3

**Q7. (Short answer)** A germline BRCA2 pathogenic variant is found in a patient's colorectal tumour–normal pair. Tumour VAF ≈ 0.48 at purity 0.6; no HRD signature; the tumour is MSS. Is the BRCA2 variant likely to be driving this tumour? Justify.

- C. AML never has germline predisposition
