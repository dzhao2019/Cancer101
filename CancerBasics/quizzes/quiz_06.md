**Q1. (Multiple choice)** Which of these was **not** one of the six hallmarks in the original 2000 paper?

- A. Evading apoptosis
- B. Sustained angiogenesis
- C. Avoiding immune destruction
- D. Limitless replicative potential

**Q2. (Short answer)** Name the main hallmark served by each of these alterations: (a) KRAS G12D, (b) TERT promoter C228T, (c) CDKN2A homozygous deletion, (d) BCL2 overexpression from t(14;18).

**Q3. (Multiple choice)** PTEN normally acts as a tumour suppressor by:

- A. Phosphorylating RB1
- B. Removing a phosphate from PIP3, opposing PI3K–AKT signalling
- C. Degrading p53
- D. Switching RAS off by stimulating its GTPase activity

**Q4. (Short answer)** Explain why a single homozygous deletion of CDKN2A can disable both the Rb and p53 pathways.

**Q5. (Multiple choice)** Knudson's evidence for the two-hit hypothesis came from the observation that hereditary retinoblastoma:

- A. Occurs only in adults
- B. Is often bilateral and earlier-onset than sporadic retinoblastoma
- C. Is caused by RB1 amplification
- D. Requires three independent mutations

**Q6. (Short answer)** List three different mechanisms by which a tumour can lose the remaining wild-type copy of a tumour suppressor, and say which one WGS alone cannot detect.

**Q7. (Multiple choice)** BRAF V600E is present in many benign moles that never become melanoma. This best illustrates:

- A. That BRAF V600E is a passenger
- B. Oncogene-induced senescence: a strong driver alone is insufficient for cancer
- C. That moles are tumour-in-normal contamination
- D. Haploinsufficiency of BRAF

**Q8. (Short answer)** Why are KRAS and BRAF hotspot mutations largely mutually exclusive in colorectal tumours? What can mutual exclusivity in cohort data tell you about unfamiliar genes?

**Q9. (Multiple choice)** In driver discovery, a gene with dN/dS significantly greater than 1 suggests:

- A. Negative (purifying) selection
- B. Neutral evolution
- C. Positive selection for protein-changing mutations — a likely driver
- D. A sequencing artifact

**Q10. (Short answer)** A gene in a cohort shows mostly truncating mutations scattered along its length, often with LOH. Classify it, and explain why targeting it with a drug is harder than targeting an oncogene. Name one alternative therapeutic strategy.
