**Q1 — 8 mutations/Mb.**
(18,000 + 2,000) / 2,500 = **8.0 mut/Mb**.
Not comparable to a panel because of: a different variant set (panels often count coding variants only, may exclude hotspots); a much smaller denominator (~1 Mb panel → noisier); different filters; and the regulatory threshold (≥ 10) being validated on a specific panel assay. Purity also affects both. *(Section 2)*

**Q2 — B.**
Frameshifts produce long novel protein sequences, a rich source of neoantigens that T cells can recognise once checkpoint brakes are released. *(Section 3)*

**Q3 — Model answer.**
MLH1/PMS2 co-loss points to an MLH1 problem. Test:
1. **BRAF V600E** — present favours sporadic.
2. **MLH1 promoter methylation** — present favours sporadic (both alleles silenced epigenetically).

If both are negative, suspect **Lynch syndrome** and refer for germline testing (Module 9). *(Section 3)*

**Q4 — C.**
POLE proofreading loss gives ultramutation with SBS10a/b, few indels and MSS status. MMR loss (B) gives many indels and MSI. Tobacco (A) gives SBS4 at lower TMB. HRD (D) does not cause ultramutation. *(Sections 3, 5; worked example)*

**Q5 — Model answer.**
**LOH** (count of large LOH regions), **TAI** (telomeric allelic imbalance) and **LST** (large-scale state transitions).
Scars are a **historical record**. If HR is later restored — most often by a BRCA1/2 **reversion mutation** under platinum or PARP inhibitor pressure — the scars remain, but the tumour can now repair breaks and resists PARP inhibitors. *(Section 4)*

**Q6 — Model answer.**
Each SNV is described by its substitution plus the 5′ and 3′ neighbouring bases. The mutated base is written as a **pyrimidine** (C or T), giving 6 substitution classes; × 4 possible 5′ bases × 4 possible 3′ bases = **96**.
A G>A on one strand is the same event as C>T on the other strand, so the trinucleotide is reverse-complemented and counted under C>T. This merges strand-equivalent events. *(Section 5)*

**Q7 — B.**
For a single sample you **fit** known reference signatures (e.g. SigProfilerAssignment, MutationalPatterns). De novo extraction needs many samples. *(Section 5)*

**Q8 — 1-c, 2-e, 3-a, 4-b, 5-d.**
SBS4 tobacco; SBS7a UV; SBS11 temozolomide; SBS88 colibactin; ID6 microhomology-mediated deletions from HR deficiency. *(Section 5 table)*

**Q9 — Model answer.**
Chromothripsis: **many clustered breakpoints** confined to one or a few chromosomes (or arms), with **copy number oscillating between two (sometimes three) states** and alternating retention and LOH — the signature of a **single catastrophic event**, shattering then random reassembly.
Gradual CIN builds changes over many divisions: fewer breakpoints per region, many different CN levels, and whole-chromosome/arm changes spread across the genome. *(Section 6)*

**Q10 — B.**
1p/19q codeletion with IDH mutation defines oligodendroglioma. +7/−10 suggests IDH-wild-type glioblastoma; 3p loss is typical of clear cell renal carcinoma; del(17p) is a prognostic marker in CLL and myeloma. *(Section 6)*
